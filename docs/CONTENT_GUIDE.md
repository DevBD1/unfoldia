# Contributing a learning activity

Propose one useful learning slice before a whole course. Include:

1. Audience, prerequisites, subject, language, and a concrete learning objective.
2. A learner action with an observable result and an explanation of why it happens.
3. A knowledge check with explained answers, including common misconceptions.
4. Sources: author, title, URL, access date, relevant claim, and license/provenance.
5. Model limits: distinguish documented facts, calculations, assumptions, and
   schematic visualization. Attractive geometry is not scientific validation.
6. Accessible alternatives and keyboard/narrow-screen behavior. Where possible,
   keep explanations and numeric experiments usable without WebGL.
7. Tests for calculations, semantic IDs, state, and legacy progress behavior;
   desktop/narrow evidence for changed interactions, with unverified areas stated.

Use original explanations and redistributable assets. Linking a textbook does not
permit copying its pages or diagrams. Do not commit purchased models, textbook
scans, or generated assets without checking rights. AI-assisted content still
requires source verification and human review; disclose material assistance and
never submit fabricated citations. See [third-party notices](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

New subjects need not use 3D. Choose the simplest representation that teaches the
relationship. Follow [architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md) for registration and deployment
routes. Stable subject/part/lesson IDs are not translated or renamed for branding.
Mark incomplete lessons and planned stages honestly; review is required before
calling a learning activity ready.
